\textbf{Penalty kicks.}
A Forward and a Goalkeeper play a penalty kick.
The Forward can shoot to the left corner or to the right corner.
If he shoots on the right, then he is always on target.
But if he shoots on the left, then he is on target only with probability \(\frac{5}{6}\).
The Goalkeeper can jump to the left corner or to the right corner.
If he jumps to the left and the ball is also on the left, then he always catches the ball.
But if he jumps to the right, then he catches the ball only with probability \(\frac{2}{3}\).
If the ball and the Goalkeeper are in different corners, then the Goalkeeper never catches the ball.
The Forward gets \(6\) points if he hits the target, \(-6\) points if he misses the target, and \(0\) points if the Goalkeeper catches the ball.
The Goalkeeper gets \(6\) points if he catches the ball, \(-6\) points if the Forward hits the target, and \(0\) points if the Forward misses.
Draw the matrices and solve the game.
(The players are interested only in expected outcomes).

\textbf{Solution.}

Let the Forward (F) choose rows and the Goalkeeper (G) choose columns. Each player has two strategies:
\[
L=\text{left corner}, \qquad R=\text{right corner}
\]

\textbf{Payoff matrix.}

\textbf{Case $(L,L)$:}
\[
u_F(L,L)=\frac{5}{6}\cdot 0 + \frac{1}{6}\cdot(-6)=-1,
\]
\[
u_G(L,L)=\frac{5}{6}\cdot 6 + \frac{1}{6}\cdot 0=5
\]

\textbf{Case $(L,R)$:}
\[
u_F(L,R)=\frac{5}{6}\cdot 6 + \frac{1}{6}\cdot(-6)=4,
\]
\[
u_G(L,R)=\frac{5}{6}\cdot(-6) + \frac{1}{6}\cdot 0=-5
\]

\textbf{Case $(R,L)$:}
\[
u_F(R,L)=6,
\]
\[
u_G(R,L)=-6
\]

\textbf{Case $(R,R)$:}
\[
u_F(R,R)=\frac{2}{3}\cdot 0 + \frac{1}{3}\cdot 6=2,
\]
\[
u_G(R,R)=\frac{2}{3}\cdot 6 + \frac{1}{3}\cdot(-6)=2
\]

Hence the payoff matrix is
\[
\begin{array}{c|cc}
 & \text{G }L & \text{G }R\\
\hline
\text{F }L & (-1,5) & (4,-5)\\
\text{F }R & (6,-6) & (2,2)
\end{array}
\]

\textbf{Pure Nash equilibriums.}

Forward's best responses:
\begin{itemize}
\item If G plays $L$: $-1 < 6$, so F prefers $R$.
\item If G plays $R$: $4 > 2$, so F prefers $L$.
\end{itemize}

Goalkeeper's best responses:
\begin{itemize}
\item If F plays $L$: $5 > -5$, so G prefers $L$.
\item If F plays $R$: $2 > -6$, so G prefers $R$.
\end{itemize}

There is no pure Nash equilibrium.

\textbf{Mixed Nash equilibrium.}

Let
\[
p=\mathbb{P}(\text{F plays }L), \qquad
q=\mathbb{P}(\text{G plays }L)
\]

\textbf{Forward indifferent.}
\[
u_F(L)=q(-1)+(1-q)4=4-5q,
\]
\[
u_F(R)=q\cdot 6+(1-q)\cdot 2=2+4q
\]
\[
4-5q=2+4q \;\Rightarrow\; q=\frac{2}{9}
\]

\textbf{Goalkeeper indifferent.}
\[
u_G(L)=p\cdot 5+(1-p)(-6)=11p-6,
\]
\[
u_G(R)=p(-5)+(1-p)2=2-7p
\]
\[
11p-6=2-7p \;\Rightarrow\; p=\frac{4}{9}
\]

That is,
\[
\text{Forward plays }L\text{ with probability }\frac{4}{9}, \quad
\text{Goalkeeper plays }L\text{ with probability }\frac{2}{9}
\]
